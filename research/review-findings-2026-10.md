# Adversarial review findings — October 2026

**Status: all 34 fixed (2026-10-12). Kept as the record of what was wrong and why.**

These are the results of a 3-skeptic adversarial review of the change set that
became commit `16b8d33` ("major_algo changes"). The review ran *before* the
commit; the terminal session that ordered it hit its usage limit while it was
reading the results, so the change set was committed without any of the fixes
being applied.

**33 defects** confirmed by at least 2 of 3 independent verifiers, plus 1 found
later by a completeness critic (S1, below).

Each `FIX` below describes what was suggested; what was actually done may
differ where the suggestion turned out to be insufficient. Two cases:

- **C6** needed more than the suggested edge-frame skip. Skipping only the
  frames the local fit extrapolates to still left frames `h+1..h+5` rejected,
  because a quadratic cannot follow ~80 g of deceleration and the segment-wide
  MAD threshold is set by the much smaller interior residuals. The fix is a
  cubic local fit plus a *local* robust threshold (each residual compared with
  its neighbours'). Mean launch error at 324 km/h: 127.5 → 16.8 km/h, with
  clutter rejection unchanged (80/80 caught in a 40-track simulation).
- **C23**'s magic-link sign-in was removed rather than repaired. It cannot
  complete without a Supabase redirect-URL allow-list entry and a deep-link
  handler, so it alerted "Magic link sent" and then never signed anyone in.

Regression tests live in `packages/cv-core/tests/test_cv_regressions.py` and
`packages/pose-biomechanics/tests/test_pose_regressions.py`.

### C1 [medium] cv-geometry packages/cv-core/src/speed.py:408 (3/3)
Physics fit has no bounce/rebound but fits 0.6 s for every sport, so ball-sport speeds are biased yet pass the 12 px gate
DESC: fit_flight_physics fits a single gravity + drag arc over max_duration_s=0.6 s. The pipeline applies it to every SportProfile. Between two hits, a tennis, table tennis, pickleball or padel ball bounces, and a squash ball rebounds off the front wall, often well inside 0.6 s. The robust soft-L1 loss absorbs the post-bounce points well enough that the RMS stays below CVPipeline.MAX_REPROJ_PX (12 px), so the biased physics result is accepted. When the fit is rejected, the plane-path drag fit (fit_launch_speed) also has no bounce model and is fed post-bounce points too, as the table-tennis case shows. Both fits were tuned for a shuttle, which never bounces before the next hit.
FAIL: Pickleball drive, 70.2 km/h true launch, camera (1,-4,2.5), anchored plane. With 0.45 s observed (no bounce) the result is exact: 70.2 km/h launch, 52.3 km/h at the net. With 0.9 s observed (bounce at ~0.5 s) it reports 79.0 km/h launch (+12.5%) and 68.2 km/h at the net (+30%), method='physics', rms 5.1 px, so it is accepted. A table-tennis drive at 57.6 km/h is reported as 52.3 km/h through the plane fallback. Script: scratchpad/review/cv-geometry/bounce.py / bounce2.py
FIX: Only fit flight up to the first bounce or rebound. Add a bounce/wall flag (or a per-sport max_duration_s) to SportProfile. Before calling fit_flight_physics and fit_launch_speed, find the first bounce: a vertical-velocity sign flip in a plane-lifted seed or a curvature break in the pixel track, or a change point in the residuals after a first fit. Then cut seg/seg_t at that frame. Alternatively, model a ground/wall contact in simulate_flight (piecewise, with restitution and friction). Also reject a physics fit whose residuals stay large over a run of trailing frames, even when the overall RMS is under 12 px.

### C2 [medium] cv-geometry packages/cv-core/src/sports.py:86 (3/3)
Net-post keypoints are placed on the sidelines for every sport; tennis/pickleball/table-tennis posts stand outside them
DESC: calibration_keypoints() puts net_post_left/right at x=0 and x=court_width for every net sport. That is only right for badminton (BWF posts stand on the doubles sidelines) and padel. Tennis post centres are 0.914 m outside the doubles sidelines (ITF Rule 1), pickleball posts are 12 in (0.305 m) outside the sidelines, and table-tennis posts are 15.25 cm outside the side lines (ITTF 2.2.3). When a user taps the real post tops, the 6-point DLT returns a badly wrong camera. The misplacement is along the net-top line, so the DLT absorbs it exactly and reprojection_error_px comes out 0.0 px, giving no warning. This is reachable via CourtCamera.from_keypoints and CVPipeline.calibrate/process_video(keypoints_px=...).
FAIL: Tennis camera at (2,-8,4) with true post-top pixels: the 6-point DLT camera centre is (3.34,-0.37,2.88), about 7.8 m off, with reprojection 0.0 px, and a point lifted onto a known plane comes back NaN. With the 4 corners only it is exact. Pickleball's centre is off by 2.4 m and its lifted point is at y=6.13 instead of 10.06. Table tennis lifts to NaN. Script: scratchpad/review/cv-geometry/net_posts.py
FIX: Add a per-sport `net_post_offset_m` field to `SportProfile`: badminton 0.0, padel 0.0 (check against the FIP rules), tennis 0.914, pickleball 0.3048, table_tennis 0.1525. Then place the posts at (-offset, net_y_m, net_height_m) and (court_width_m + offset, net_y_m, net_height_m). As an alternative, rename the keypoints to "net top at the sideline" and update the docs, ARCHITECTURE.md and the UI to tell users to tap that spot instead of the post tops. Also add a test that projects the true post positions for each sport and checks the recovered camera centre.

### C3 [medium] cv-geometry packages/cv-core/src/sports.py:108 (3/3)
Table-tennis court frame puts z=0 at the table top, but flight anchors map the players' feet onto that plane
DESC: For table_tennis the 'court' is the table (2.74 x 1.525 m), so the calibrated ground plane z=0 is the table surface, about 0.76 m above the floor. orchestrator._run_real_pipeline builds each flight's motion-plane anchor as camera.image_to_ground(ankle midpoint). For this sport that intersects the feet ray with the table-top plane instead of the floor, so the anchors are pulled toward the camera by metres. The profile has no floor offset, and image_to_ground ('pixels on the ground (e.g. feet)') has no way to account for one. Every anchored table-tennis flight is therefore lifted onto the wrong plane.
FAIL: Camera 3 m behind the near end, 1 m above the table. Near player's feet at (0.5,-1.0) map to anchor (0.03,-1.86), and far player's feet at (1.0,3.9) map to (0.31,0.92). A ball at (0.72,1.2,0.3) on the true shot plane lifts exactly with the true anchors, but lifts to (0.15,-0.61,0.60) with the anchors the orchestrator would pass, about 1.9 m off. Script: scratchpad/review/cv-geometry/tt_anchor.py

Give `CourtCamera` an `image_to_plane(pixels, z)` method that intersects `rays()` with the plane at that height (or call `lift_to_plane` with normal (0,0,1)). In orchestrator.py:131, build feet anchors at `z = sport.floor_z_m` instead of with `image_to_ground`. Also update the `image_to_ground` and `motion_plane` docstrings to say the anchor is on the floor, not the court plane.

### C4 [low] cv-geometry packages/cv-core/src/speed.py:305 (2/3)
Hit-time extrapolation silently switches off at k*v*tau >= 0.9, so a faster flight is reported as far slower
DESC: When hit_time_s is given, v0 is extrapolated back by v0/(1 - k v0 tau) only if k*v0*tau < 0.9. Above that threshold the function returns the speed at the first sample unchanged. LaunchFit.speed_ms is still documented as the speed at the hit time, and the plane-path pipeline reports it as launch speed. The output is therefore discontinuous and non-monotonic: just below the threshold it is about 10x the first-sample speed (clamped to the sport maximum), and just above it drops to 1x. This is reached when the first usable sample comes several frames after the hit (racket or body occlusion) and the hit frame or plane scale is slightly off.
FAIL: Badminton, first usable sample tau=0.10 s after the hit, exact drag flights. A first-sample speed of 148 km/h is reported as a 565 km/h launch, while 151 km/h (a faster flight) is reported as 151.2 km/h. Script: scratchpad/review/cv-geometry/extrap.py
FIX: Remove the fallback to v1. One option is to fit the speed at the hit time directly: in sse(), model s(t) = s0 + ln(1 + k*v0*(t + tau))/k with tau = t_first - hit_time_s (clamped to >= 0). The search over [lo, hi] then returns a bounded speed at the hit time that rises with the data, and the division goes away. If the closed-form extrapolation is kept, make it saturate instead of switching off: when 1 - k*v0*tau <= 0.1, return hi (or return None / mark the launch unreliable), not v0. Either way, consider capping tau to a few frames and flagging the launch as low-confidence when it is larger.

### C5 [low] cv-geometry packages/cv-core/src/court.py:192 (3/3)
6-point DLT is used unconditionally even when ill-conditioned (side-on camera near net height)
DESC: from_keypoints always prefers the 6-point DLT whenever both net-post tops are supplied. The DLT degenerates when the camera lies on, or near, the line through the two post tops (points on a plane plus a line through the camera centre). That is exactly the side-on, at-the-net coaching view. In that view the two post tops project a few tens of pixels apart on one vertical, so ordinary tap noise wrecks the vertical axis. No conditioning check or fallback to the better-conditioned 4-corner homography plus focal path is made.
FAIL: Badminton camera side-on at (-6,6.7,1.3), f=1000: the post tops project to (960,409) and (960,431). With 2 px tap noise, the median error lifting a point onto a known plane is 0.59 m for the 6-point DLT versus 0.085 m for the 4-corner path on the same taps (0.24 m vs 0.055 m at 1 px). Script: scratchpad/review/cv-geometry/sideon.py
FIX: In `from_keypoints`, gate the 6-point path on conditioning. Either have `fit_projection` return the ratio of the second-smallest to the largest singular value of the normalised design matrix, or check directly whether the camera lies near the post-top line, for example by testing whether the two post-top pixels are nearly collinear with the vanishing direction of the net line, or are only a few tens of pixels apart. When the 6-point problem is ill-conditioned, fall back to `fit_homography` + `projection_from_homography`. A more robust alternative is to always build P from the ground homography and use the post tops only to refine the focal length or the vertical column by least squares, keeping whichever candidate has the lower held-out error. Either way, expose which path was used, or flag the poor conditioning, instead of relying on `reprojection_error_px`, which stays near zero for a 6-point DLT.

### C6 [high] cv-tracking packages/cv-core/src/trajectory.py:122 (3/3)
rectify_trajectory deletes the first detections after every fast hit, which corrupts launch speed
DESC: The outlier pass (lines 92-111) predicts each frame from a local quadratic fitted to its neighbours. At a segment start (the hit frame), every neighbour is on one side, so the prediction is a one-sided extrapolation of a sharply decelerating flight. The MAD threshold is computed from the small interior residuals, so the first post-hit frames get flagged as REPLACED. The fill loop (lines 121-125) then finds no valid observation before them, sets them to MISSING and writes NaN. This happens even with noise-free detections. As a result, analyse_track's physics and drag fits lose the most informative samples (the frames right after contact) and have to extrapolate back to the hit. Because those frames also get confidence 0, get_summary/run_benchmark's detection_rate undercounts real detections.
FAIL: Near-court smash: hitter at (3.0, 2.5, 2.5), camera OFFSET_CAM, 60 fps, perfect detections. rectify_trajectory(pix, hit_frames=[h]).status[h:h+7] is [-1 -1 -1 -1 0 0 0] at 324 km/h and [-1 -1 -1 0 ...] at 252 km/h. With 1 px detector noise, analyse_track's mean |launch error| over 15 seeds is 127.5 km/h at 324 km/h (63.5 km/h at 252 km/h). Keeping the observed post-hit detections gives 7.4 (4.3) km/h. Over 200 random 40-100 m/s near-court flights, hit+0 was dropped 90% of the time and on average 3.6 leading frames were removed (scratchpad/review/cv-tracking/edge_simple2.py, edge_simple3.py, rect_launch.py).
FIX: In `rectify_trajectory`'s outlier pass, only test frames whose neighbour window has valid frames on both sides, i.e. `near.min() < f < near.max()`. Otherwise, skip the frame or scale the threshold with how far the fit is extrapolating. In the fill loop, an edge frame flagged REPLACED that can't be interpolated should get its original observation back (status OBSERVED, position = raw) instead of becoming MISSING/NaN. Add a regression test that runs a `simulate_flight` drag trajectory at 60-90 m/s with `hit_frames` and asserts that `status[h:h+3] == OBSERVED`.

### C7 [high] cv-tracking packages/cv-core/src/pipeline.py:348 (3/3)
With hit_frames=None, coarse rectification across the unknown hits erases most trajectory turns
DESC: When no hits are passed in (the default in process_video, which also supplies the orchestrator's shuttle turns for fuse_hits), analyse_track runs rectify_trajectory(raw, max_gap=4) with no hit breaks and then looks for turns. Without breaks, the local-quadratic outlier pass treats each reversal as outliers and replaces the frames around it with fitted values (on average 5.6 of the 7 frames around each hit), smoothing the turn away. The turns that remain are often shifted by about 6 frames. The flight split, hit_time and launch fit then use the wrong frame.
FAIL: Perfect detections of a lift -> smash -> lift rally with true hits at [60, 96]. find_trajectory_turns on the detections returns [95]. After the coarse rectification every frame in 92-100 is REPLACED and the only turn is [90]. analyse_track(pix, ...) reports stroke_detected at 90 and one flight starting at 90 with launch 161 km/h, while the real flight from frame 96 is a 72 km/h lift. Over 60 simulated noise-free rallies (240 hits), coarse-rectify-then-turns finds 61 hits with 49 spurious turns; turns on the raw detections find 150 with 0 spurious (turns_simple.py, turns3.py, turns4.py).
FIX: Do not run the outlier pass across hits you don't know yet. Detect turn candidates on the detections after gap-filling only, e.g. `rectify_trajectory(raw, max_gap=4, iterations=0)`, which inpaints short gaps without replacing frames. Then filter out candidates caused by single-frame outliers, for example by requiring the turn to persist when the candidate frame is removed, or by keeping a candidate only if splitting there lowers the local-fit residual. Finally, rectify with the surviving candidates as `hit_frames`. Alternatively, exempt frames within about ±3 of any high-angle candidate from REPLACED marking in the break-less pass.

### C8 [medium] cv-tracking packages/cv-core/src/pipeline.py:387 (3/3)
Physics-fit positions are extrapolated over the whole segment, so frames with no shuttle get speeds
DESC: fit_flight_physics fits only up to 0.6 s after the hit, but returns simulated positions for every frame in [a, b). analyse_track writes them all into metric[a:b] and derives per-frame speeds from them. For the last flight of a video (b = len(pos)), or any segment that covers dead time, the simulated shuttle keeps falling through the floor at terminal velocity. Every frame then gets a finite speed (about 23 km/h) even though shuttle_x/y is NaN. get_summary's avg/min speeds are skewed, and the orchestrator's cv_results report speed_kmh for frames where shuttle_detected is False.
FAIL: A 60 m/s anchored flight starting at frame 30 (0.8 s), followed by 3 s with no detections. Method is physics, launch 215 km/h. All 175 tail frames have NaN position, but 174 have speed_kmh of about 23-24 km/h. get_summary avg_speed_kmh is 28.5 against 47.8 over the actual flight frames (tail_extrap.py).
FIX: In analyse_track, write the physics positions only where the shuttle was seen, as the plane path does. Either use `metric[a:b][ok] = physics.positions_m[ok]`, or limit the simulated positions to frames between the first and last finite observation of the segment (optionally also dropping frames where simulated z < 0). Alternatively, have fit_flight_physics return NaN for times after the last observation. This keeps speeds NaN in dead time, matching shuttle_x/y.

### C9 [medium] cv-tracking packages/cv-core/src/pipeline.py:231 (2/3)
process_frame raises AttributeError (_live_plane) after calibrate() or process_video() sets the camera

### C10 [medium] cv-tracking packages/cv-core/src/tracker.py:158 (3/3)
The tracker does not reset consecutive_misses on loss, so a re-initialised track dies on its first missed frame
DESC: When a track is dropped (consecutive_misses > MAX_OCCLUSION_FRAMES), the code sets _initialized=False and hits=0 but leaves consecutive_misses at 6. The next detection re-initialises the track (_init_at) without clearing the counter, so if the following frame has no detection the count goes to 7 and the track is dropped again at once. With intermittent detections after the first loss in a session, the tracker never coasts, never reaches MIN_HITS (so gating and is_valid never engage), and re-initialises with zero velocity on every detection. The live hit cue also never fires, because _live_track is cleared every other frame. This regressed from HEAD, which never de-initialised the track.
FAIL: A fresh tracker fed detections on alternate frames outputs a position every frame (it coasts through the gaps). The same tracker, after an earlier rally ended with 6 empty frames, outputs ['100','--','120','--','140',...]: None on every missed frame, indefinitely (tracker_stale.py).
FIX: In tracker.py, reset consecutive_misses to 0 (and clear _rejected) whenever a track starts. The simplest way is to do it inside _init_at, so both the fresh-init path and the re-acquire path get it. Alternatively, set self.consecutive_misses = 0 next to self.hits = 1 at line 129, or call self.reset() when the track is dropped at lines 157-160 (reset() also clears history).

### C11 [medium] cv-tracking packages/cv-core/src/pipeline.py:240 (3/3)
Live speed preview reports a false spike when a new track starts after the old one was lost
DESC: When the tracker returns None (track lost), process_frame clears only _live_track. SpeedCalculator keeps the _prev_pos of the old track. When a new track starts elsewhere, the jump between the two tracks is scored as one frame of motion, clamped to max_speed_kmh and pushed into the EMA. StreamingPipeline then emits it as a SPEED_UPDATE. The rewrite added a NaN guard for the EMA but kept this. Skipping NaN lifts also leaves multi-frame gaps that are scored the same way.
FAIL: A slow track (2 px/frame, about 3.3 km/h) ends, 30 empty frames follow, then a new slow track starts 1200 px away. The live speed_kmh sequence after re-acquisition is [254.2, 139.8, 76.9, 42.3, ...] km/h, against a true 3.3 km/h (live_spike.py).

Then the first sample of a new track only seeds the position and is never scored as motion.

### C13 [low] cv-tracking packages/cv-core/src/tracker.py:149 (3/3)
On re-acquisition, velocity is computed as if the two rejected detections were one frame apart
DESC: The re-acquire check allows any number of missed frames between the remembered rejected detection and the current one (the threshold scales with consecutive_misses + 1). Velocity, however, is always set to (z - prev) / dt, so a 2- or 3-frame gap doubles or triples the initial velocity. The next coasted prediction then overshoots. The threshold also scales with misses counted since the last accepted detection, not since the rejected one, which inflates the tolerance.
FAIL: Shuttle reversed to -25 px/frame. The first post-hit detection is gated out, then 1 or 2 frames have no detection, then a second gated-out detection triggers re-acquisition. get_velocity gives -50 or -75 px/frame instead of -25. The next update is ungated, so it converges, but a miss in that frame emits a position 25-50 px off (reacq_vel.py).
FIX: Store how old the rejected detection is: keep `self._rejected_age = 0` when setting `_rejected`, and increment it on every frame that does not re-acquire. At re-acquisition, use `gap = self._rejected_age + 1` for both the threshold (`REACQUIRE_PX * gap`) and the velocity (`self.x[2:4] = (z - prev) / (gap * self.dt)`). Optionally widen the initial velocity covariance when gap > 1. Add a test with a one-frame gap between the two rejected detections.

### C14 [medium] cv-tracknet packages/cv-core/src/train_tracknet.py:82 (2/3)
Empty validation split makes val_loss 0.0, so only the epoch-0 weights are saved and LR is cut on a fake plateau
DESC: When the dataset root holds a single match directory, tracknet_dataset.py:53 sets n_val=0 and split='val' returns no samples. train() then computes val_loss = sum(<empty>)/max(0,1) = 0.0 every epoch. FAIL: Synthetic root/Professional/match1 (1 clip, 30 frames). train('data1', epochs=6, width=0.125) printed `train 28 val 0`. Every epoch showed `val 0.00000`, and LR dropped 1e-3 -> 5e-4 at epoch 4 while train loss kept falling (0.304 -> 0.272). tracknet.pt holds the epoch-0 weights and tracknet_history.json reports best_val_loss 0.0. ### C15 [high] pose packages/pose-biomechanics/src/stroke_events.py:296 (3/3)
fuse_hits drops a real hit at rally boundaries because the 'no double hit' rule runs over the whole video
DESC: Rule 4 (same side can't hit twice in a row) runs once over every hit in the video. The orchestrator calls fuse_hits on all CV turns for the whole clip and never splits it into rallies. In badminton the rally winner serves next. So when the tracked player ends a rally with a winner and then serves, there are two consecutive 'player' hits and the less confident one is discarded. The same happens to 'opponent' hits when the opponent wins a rally: every opponent hit has confidence 0.5, so the later one, the serve, is always dropped. The lost hit is also removed from cv_pipe.reanalyse(hit_frames=...), so flights are segmented wrongly, and the stroke disappears from the session output.
FAIL: fps=60, pose events at 31, 149, 451, 569; shuttle turns at 30, 90, 150 (rally 1, player wins), then 450, 510, 570 (rally 2, player serves). fuse_hits returns 5 hits and drops the player's serve at 450 ([(30,player),(90,opponent),(150,player),(510,opponent),(570,player)]). Demonstrated in review/pose/fuse_rally.py.
FIX: Only apply the "no same side twice in a row" rule within a rally. Split the sorted hits into rallies wherever the gap between consecutive hits is longer than the longest normal shot interval (about 2-2.5 s for badminton, taken from the sport profile), or wherever the shuttle is unseen or still for a long stretch. Then run the collapse step inside each rally. The minimal version of this: only merge two same-side hits when `hit.frame - cleaned[-1].frame <= max_shot_gap_s * fps`, and otherwise keep both.

### C16 [high] pose packages/pose-biomechanics/src/keypoints.py:176 (2/2)
